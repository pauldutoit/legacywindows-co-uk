## Sash and case window quotes for Edinburgh's New Town, Marchmont and Bruntsfield

The sash and case window is the defining architectural element of Edinburgh — from the Georgian six-over-six sashes of the New Town to the Victorian two-over-two units in Marchmont and Bruntsfield tenement flats. Because the New Town and Old Town are a UNESCO World Heritage Site, almost every job here is either a like-for-like timber restoration or a slim-glazed timber replacement under Listed Building Consent. ## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need Listed Building Consent for a New Town sash replacement?**
Almost always yes. If your building is A or B-listed (most of the New Town is A-listed) or falls inside the World Heritage Site, any change to a street-facing sash requires LBC from the City of Edinburgh Council. Rear elevations are sometimes granted more latitude, but even there a like-for-like timber replacement is generally the expectation.

**Slim double glazing versus draught-sealing existing sashes — which is worth it?**
Draught-sealing an existing sound sash is typically £370 to £560 per window and cuts heat loss by around 40%. A full slim-glazed timber replacement is £1,300 to £2,280 and cuts heat loss by 65-70% while addressing rattling and rot. Marchmont tenement owners often start with draught-sealing on rears and slim glazing on the front only.

**Do slim units meet Section 6?**
Slim 11-14mm units achieve around Uw 1.7 W/m²K, above the 1.4 target. Scottish Building Standards permit relaxation for listed and conservation buildings where full compliance would harm character — this is routinely accepted in Edinburgh.
