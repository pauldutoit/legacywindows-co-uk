## Frequently asked questions

**Does secondary glazing need Listed Building Consent in Birmingham?**
Usually yes on a Grade I or II listed building, though local authorities recognise it as reversible and low-impact. The application is far simpler than replacing the primary sash or casement. Installers in our network prepare the drawings and heritage statement as part of the quote.

**Will secondary glazing satisfy Part L on a listed Jewellery Quarter conversion?**
Listed buildings are largely exempt from Part L where full compliance would harm the historic fabric. Secondary glazing typically brings the combined unit down to around Uw 1.7 W/m²K, which is the accepted heritage benchmark used by Birmingham City Council heritage officers.

**How much noise reduction can I expect on a Digbeth apartment above the ring road?**
A 100mm cavity between the primary sash and a 6.4mm acoustic laminate secondary pane delivers around 45dB reduction, which is enough to drop the noise of the Middleway from disruptive to background.
